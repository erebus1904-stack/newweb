PMP Online Exam vs Test Center in 2026: OnVUE Risks and Checklist

Choose your exam setting, check the weak points, and know where to turn if the technology fails.

Last reviewed: September 8, 2026Published by PassGrid Editorial Team.Editorial Policy

Short answer

A test center may fit better if home technology or privacy is uncertain; PMI currently recommends test-center delivery. Online exam may fit with a controlled setup and difficult travel. Passing the system test is a readiness check, not a guarantee against later technical problems. Choose based on your circumstances, not one person's experience.

When online delivery may be the lower-risk choice

Online delivery can be practical when you have already run the current official system test on the exact computer and connection you will use, can reserve a private space for the full appointment window, and can prevent other people, alerts, deliveries, pets, or workplace interruptions from entering the process.

Do not choose online only to avoid travel. Choose it when the complete environment is more predictable than the trip to a center. A fast connection on an ordinary day is not the same as a controlled exam setup.

When a test center may be the lower-risk choice

A test center can be the more conservative option when your internet or power is inconsistent, your available computer cannot pass the current system test, your room cannot stay private, or the possibility of a home interruption would distract you throughout the exam.

Check seat availability before building the entire study calendar around one location. Also plan transport, arrival buffer, identification, and any accommodations through the official channels. PassGrid does not define admission or personal-item rules because those instructions can change.

Run this check before scheduling

  1. Confirm that your PMI application status allows scheduling.
  2. Open the current Pearson VUE page for PMI and compare available delivery options for your exam and location.
  3. If considering online delivery, run the current official system test on the same device, network, and room setup.
  4. Read the appointment-specific identification, check-in, break, room, and support instructions rather than relying on an older forum post.
  5. Choose the route with fewer uncontrolled risks, then save the confirmation and support route.

PassGrid planning advice: decide whether you could tolerate a delayed appointment before choosing a route. A tight employment deadline, long-distance travel, or a computer you cannot administer can make the same option sensible for one candidate and impractical for another. Neither delivery method eliminates all risk.

OnVUE checklist: equipment, room, and check-in

The PMI-specific Pearson VUE requirements say to:

  • Test the actual computer and network; use one screen, a webcam, microphone, and speakers. VPNs and corporate or public networks are prohibited.
  • Keep the room private and the desk cleared; confirm acceptable identification and any approved accommodations.
  • Start check-in 30 minutes early and complete the identity and room checks.

PassGrid planning advice: inspect these requirements before buying equipment or booking time off. Resolve conflicts between your setup and the rules early. A spare computer is a pre-appointment planning option, not permission to switch devices during an active exam. Recheck any replacement setup through the official process.

What to do if OnVUE freezes or disconnects

Pearson VUE's Getting Help instructions specify:

  1. Use the proctor chat. Proctors cannot pause or extend the exam or troubleshoot your equipment.
  2. For a frozen or disconnected computer, close and relaunch OnVUE from Downloads.
  3. If trouble continues, use the PMI program support page.

Do not record or share the exam screen, including during a failure. Use a phone only with explicit proctor permission, and leave camera view only when an approved break is confirmed.

After the session: prepare a support record

The following is PassGrid's documentation advice, not a promise of reimbursement. Once the restricted exam session is over, write a factual timeline: appointment time and time zone, check-in progress, whether the exam started, symptoms, and actions already taken. Exclude question text and screenshots of exam content.

Contact the official support channel, ask for a case number, and retain the correspondence. Ask who owns the review, what evidence they need, when to follow up, and whether another booking would affect the case. Do not assume a technical incident automatically produces a refund, free attempt, or immediate reschedule.

Practice the environment, not only the questions

Use the PMP mock exam in the setting you expect to use. If you are leaning online, practice on the same desk and computer without notifications or interruptions. If you are leaning toward a center, simulate the travel start time, a neutral desk, limited personal items, and the pacing plan you will follow away from home.

This rehearsal is a stress and logistics exercise. It does not reproduce Pearson VUE software or official test-center procedures, and it should not be treated as an operational rule source.

Frequently asked questions

Does a passed system test guarantee a successful online exam?

No. It checks a setup at a particular time. It cannot make later power, connection, or software conditions predictable. Include those uncertainties in your decision rather than treating a successful rehearsal as insurance.

Should one bad OnVUE report make everyone choose a test center?

No. An individual report can reveal a scenario worth planning for, but cannot establish a failure rate. A center moves equipment responsibility away from you; it adds travel and seat-availability constraints. Compare those tradeoffs for your appointment.

Should I immediately pay for another exam after a technical failure?

First clarify the appointment status and available options with official support. A pending technical review is not the same as a recorded failed exam. Keep any written decision so that later advice is based on your case, not a forum example.
