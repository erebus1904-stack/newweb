Are PMP Exam Dumps Allowed? PMI Exam Security Rules and Safe Alternatives

A large question bank is not automatically a dump. The risk starts when material claims to reproduce live questions, depends on candidate recall, or distributes confidential exam content.

Published and reviewed: August 28, 2026By PassGrid Editorial Team.Editorial Policy

Short answer

No. PMI prohibits receiving, using, discussing, or distributing confidential exam questions and answers. A practice set becomes risky when it claims to reproduce live questions, uses recalled content, or promises a pass through actual exam items. Use official materials and original practice questions that teach the Exam Content Outline without copying the exam.

What counts as a PMP exam dump?

An exam dump is a collection presented as actual, recalled, reconstructed, or leaked exam content rather than independently written practice. It may be sold as a file, a subscription, a chat group, a video, or a supposed simulator. The format does not determine the violation; the source and claim do.

A provider does not need to use the word "dump" to be risky. Phrases such as "real exam questions," "latest live items," "100% same as the exam," or "guaranteed pass from current questions" should trigger scrutiny. A seller that asks recent candidates to submit remembered wording is also describing a process built around confidential content.

Legitimate practice questions vs. exam dumps

SignalLegitimate practiceDump risk
SourceAuthor-created scenarios based on public objectives and concepts.Recalled, leaked, copied, or unexplained live-exam content.
MarketingPromises practice, feedback, and skill development.Promises actual current questions or a guaranteed pass.
ExplanationMaps reasoning to a public ECO task and identifies assumptions.Focuses on memorizing the reported answer and wording.
UpdatesRewrites coverage when PMI changes the ECO.Claims to refresh from recent candidate recollections.
Candidate conductNever asks users to describe live exam items.Requests screenshots, remembered questions, or answer keys after testing.

A realistic question can still be legitimate. Similarity in topic, role, or decision pattern is expected when materials cover the same public outline. The warning is a claim that the wording or answer came from a current secured exam.

What PMI's exam security rules prohibit

PMI's Candidate Security Acknowledgment requires candidates to protect confidential exam questions, content, and answers. The restriction covers discussing or disclosing content to a person or company and receiving confidential content from another source. It applies beyond the test session: posting a remembered question afterward is not made acceptable because the candidate has finished.

Using a third party does not remove responsibility. Buying a file, joining a private group, asking an AI tool to reconstruct candidate memories, or sharing recalled questions with a training provider can all cross the same confidentiality boundary. "I did not create the dump" is not a dependable defense for knowingly using it.

How PMI can respond

PMI says it uses daily data forensics to evaluate exam results and detect unusual performance patterns. A review does not prove misconduct by itself, but it means enforcement is not limited to catching someone with notes during a proctored session.

Potential actions described by PMI include invalidating a result, revoking a certification, restricting future testing, and pursuing legal enforcement. PMI may also investigate providers and websites associated with unauthorized content. Because sanctions can affect both an exam result and a credential, an apparently cheap shortcut carries a much larger professional risk.

What to do after accidental exposure

Accidental exposure can happen when a search result, unsolicited message, or study group suddenly presents material as a real exam item. The safest response is to stop using the source rather than trying to determine whether every question is authentic.

  1. Close the material. Do not continue reading, download the full set, or test whether the answers are accurate.
  2. Do not forward it. Warning another candidate by sending the file still distributes the content.
  3. Record limited context. Keep the page address, seller name, date, and how it was presented without copying confidential question text.
  4. Remove access. Leave the group, cancel the unauthorized source, and delete downloaded copies where appropriate.
  5. Report the concern. Use PMI's exam security or customer support route and provide the limited identifying context requested by PMI.
  6. Return to clean materials. Continue with official references and independently written practice questions.

If you are unsure whether prior use affects an application or exam, explain the facts accurately to PMI. Do not invent certainty about what the material contained and do not publish the suspected questions while seeking advice.

How to report suspected PMP dumps

Start with PMI's official Exam Security page and current contact options. Report the source, URL, account or provider name, date observed, and the claim used to market the material. PMI can decide what additional evidence is useful. Avoid attaching or reproducing a complete question bank unless PMI specifically requests a secure submission.

Reporting should be factual. A suspicious title or low-quality practice set is not proof of stolen content. Describe the exact claim, such as a promise of current live questions, without publicly accusing an individual of theft when the source cannot be verified.

Safe alternatives that improve exam readiness

  • Use the active ECO: Map study and practice to the public tasks, domains, and enabling objectives.
  • Choose original practice: Look for explanations, decision rules, and transparent authorship rather than live-item claims.
  • Practice under time: Use new questions and a realistic session length so the score measures reasoning rather than recall.
  • Review mistakes: Classify concept gaps, role confusion, missed conditions, and premature action.
  • Verify policy facts: Check PMI directly for format, eligibility, security, and scheduling rules.

PassGrid's question banks are designed as original practice aligned to public learning objectives. They are not presented as recalled live PMP or CAPM questions. The goal is to train a reusable decision process, not recognition of secured wording.

AI does not make restricted content safe

An AI-generated question can be a useful study tool when the prompt uses public concepts and asks for an original scenario. It becomes unsafe when the prompt contains recalled live questions, a purchased dump, or instructions to reproduce current exam content. Reformatting confidential content does not change its source.

AI also cannot certify that a pasted question is legitimate. If a model says an item "looks like" PMP, that is not evidence of authorization. Use AI for explanation and original variation, then verify concepts against official and reputable sources.

Common questions

Are all unofficial PMP practice questions prohibited?

No. Independently written third-party practice can be legitimate. Evaluate its source, marketing claims, explanation quality, and alignment with the active ECO.

Can candidates discuss general exam experience?

General comments about preparation, pacing, or publicly documented format are different from disclosing secured questions and answers. Avoid wording, options, answer keys, and reconstructing item counts from memory as if they were official distributions.

Does deleting a dump make prior use acceptable?

Deletion prevents further use or distribution but does not rewrite what happened. If the exposure may be material, contact PMI and describe the facts accurately.
