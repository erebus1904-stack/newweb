How to Schedule the CAPM Exam: Pearson VUE Locations and Online Testing

After PMI accepts your application and you pay the exam fee, the next task is choosing an appointment. This guide explains the scheduling path, how to check Pearson VUE locations and seat availability, and when secure online testing is the better fit.

Schedule your CAPM exam in five steps

  1. Confirm that PMI has accepted your application. Scheduling begins after the application is accepted and the exam fee is paid. Use the official PMI CAPM page to verify the current exam overview and candidate process.
  2. Pay the CAPM exam fee through your PMI account. Keep the payment confirmation and make sure the name on your PMI profile matches the identification you plan to use on exam day.
  3. Open the scheduling link from your PMI account. PMI exam appointments are delivered through Pearson VUE. The official Pearson VUE page for PMI exams provides access to scheduling, seat availability, test-center search, and online testing information.
  4. Compare delivery method, dates, and locations. Search test centers you could realistically reach, then compare those appointments with the online option. Do not select a date only because it is the first one shown. Allow enough time to finish your study plan and complete realistic timed practice.
  5. Review the confirmation before you finish. Check the exam, date, local time or time zone, delivery method, location, and name. Save the appointment confirmation where you can find it. Then build your final-week checklist from the instructions attached to that appointment.

PMI currently describes the CAPM exam as 150 questions in 180 minutes and offers a Pearson VUE test center or secure online delivery. The content does not become easier because you choose one format. The scheduling decision should protect concentration and reduce preventable exam-day risk.

Check Pearson VUE seat availability before booking

A location search and an actual available appointment are not the same thing. Start with Pearson VUE's test-center search, but continue to seat availability before making travel plans. A nearby center may not offer the date you need, and the first available date may be at a different location. Search more than one realistic center and compare weekday, weekend, morning, and afternoon options when they are offered.

Availability can change as candidates book or move appointments. Treat the schedule shown during booking as current availability, not a permanent timetable. If a particular date matters, finish the booking and verify the confirmation rather than relying on an earlier search result. Also check the exact address, travel time, parking or transit, and the time you must arrive.

Choose online testing or a test center

Choose secure online testing when you have a private room, reliable internet, a compatible computer, and full control over interruptions. It can reduce travel and may provide a more useful appointment time. That convenience comes with responsibility: your room, device, connection, webcam, microphone, and check-in setup all become part of the testing environment.

Choose a Pearson VUE test center when environmental stability matters more than travel convenience. A test center provides the computer, controlled room, check-in process, and on-site staff. You still need to plan the route and arrive as instructed, but you remove many home risks such as unstable Wi-Fi, household interruptions, desk-clearing concerns, and unexpected software conflicts.

Use a simple decision test. Online is reasonable only if your registration name and ID match, your room can remain private, your desk can meet the rules, your computer and internet are dependable, and nobody will interrupt you. If one of those conditions is uncertain and cannot be fixed, a test center is usually the more predictable choice. If a center requires difficult travel, compare that travel risk with the online risk before booking.

Prepare your ID and check-in details early

Do not wait until exam week to compare your registration name with your identification. If you use multiple name formats or recently changed your name, resolve the mismatch before the appointment. Check the current identification and check-in instructions attached to your scheduling flow because the required process can differ by delivery method and location.

For a test center, confirm what you may bring, what must be stored, and when to arrive. For online testing, prepare how you will present your ID and room during check-in. Your goal is a routine check-in that consumes as little attention as possible.

Test the online setup under realistic conditions

Normal browsing does not prove that a computer is ready for online proctoring. Use Pearson VUE's current system test and instructions on the same computer, connection, room, and equipment you intend to use. Close unnecessary applications, connect to reliable power, and avoid a setup that depends on a weak wireless signal.

Run a timed practice block in that room. During the rehearsal, remove your phone, prevent notifications, and ask everyone nearby not to enter. This tests the environment as well as the device. If you remain worried about the connection or room after the rehearsal, reconsider the test-center option while appointments are still available.

Confirm the current policy before you change an appointment

Scheduling, rescheduling, cancellation, identification, and online-testing rules can change. Do not rely on an old forum post, video, or study guide for a deadline or fee. Sign in through your PMI scheduling path and confirm the current terms shown for your appointment. Use the official PMI CAPM page and Pearson VUE PMI page above when you need the current process.

Before changing an appointment, write down the existing date, time zone, delivery method, and confirmation number. Read the terms before submitting the change, then verify that a new confirmation was issued. If the account display and confirmation disagree, resolve that conflict before assuming the appointment moved successfully.

Build the final week around the appointment

Once the appointment is confirmed, practice under conditions close to the selected format. For online delivery, use the same desk and complete a timed set without checking another device. For a test center, practice at the same time of day as the appointment and plan the route with enough margin. For both formats, train your pacing: 150 questions in 180 minutes averages about 72 seconds per question.

Create one short checklist for the night before and one for exam day. Include the confirmed time, time zone, ID, route or room setup, required arrival or check-in steps, and the official contact path if something is wrong. Good scheduling is not only obtaining a seat. It removes logistics from working memory so you can focus on the CAPM question in front of you.
