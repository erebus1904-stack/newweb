PMP 35-Hour Training Rules Change December 1, 2026: What Still Qualifies?

The change narrows who may provide eligible live instruction. It does not eliminate every self-paced course or invalidate every hour earned before the effective date.

Published and reviewed: September 1, 2026By PassGrid Editorial Team.Editorial Policy

Short answer

Starting December 1, 2026, a live instructor-led PMP class is eligible for the 35-hour training requirement only when it is delivered by a PMI Authorized Training Partner, a China Registered Education Provider, or an eligible accredited higher-education program. PMI still lists self-paced courses from any organization as an eligible route. Training completed before the change remains eligible under the earlier rules, and an active CAPM certification also fulfills the PMP training requirement.

What changes on December 1, 2026?

PMI's current PMP certification page gives a specific effective date. Starting on December 1, 2026, live classes taught by an instructor, whether online or in person, are eligible only through defined provider pathways. The eligible pathways are a PMI Authorized Training Partner (ATP), a China Registered Education Provider (REP), or an eligible accredited higher education program, including PMI Global Accreditation Center programs.

This is narrower than the prior rule for live instruction. It is a provider-eligibility change, not an exam-format change and not a general ban on independent learning. The 35-hour total still belongs to PMP application eligibility; it should not be confused with the 35 Education PDUs required within the separate PMP renewal cycle.

Does a Udemy course still count for PMP?

A Udemy course delivered as genuine self-paced, on-demand training may still qualify because PMI continues to allow self-paced courses from any organization. The platform name and price do not decide the result. The course still needs to provide the required training, align with the current PMP scope, include the expected assessment, and produce records that support the hours claimed.

Do not extend that answer to every listing. A short course marketed with “35 hours” in its title may not document 35 hours of relevant training. A live cohort sold through a marketplace remains live instruction and should be checked against the new provider rule. Keep the delivery format, syllabus, completion evidence, provider identity, and dates rather than relying on a storefront screenshot alone.

What happens to training completed before December 1?

PMI's transition guidance says training completed before the provider update remains eligible under the requirements in effect when it was completed. This protects completed training; it should not be read as a promise about a course purchased but not finished before the date.

If a non-ATP live course crosses the effective date, obtain a written answer from PMI before assuming that enrollment or partial attendance preserves eligibility. The safest evidence is a completion certificate showing that the qualifying hours were completed before the rule changed. Do not alter course dates or ask a provider to backdate a certificate.

How to verify a live provider

  1. Identify the delivery organization. A trainer's PMP credential does not by itself make the organization an ATP.
  2. Search PMI's directory. Match the organization and course to the current Authorized Training Partner listing.
  3. Check the delivery format. A scheduled instructor session is live even when it uses Zoom or another online platform.
  4. Confirm the completion date. The transition treatment depends on when eligible training was completed.
  5. Retain the answer. If PMI support resolves an unusual academic, employer, or mixed-format case, save the written response.

Documents to keep for an application audit

Keep the completion certificate, provider name, course title, training dates, delivery format, syllabus or outline, and evidence of the hours completed. For a live course after December 1, also retain evidence that the delivering organization used an eligible pathway. A receipt proves payment, not completion, so keep it with rather than instead of the completion record.

Use the same information in the PMP application. Avoid rounding, combining unrelated courses without records, or describing general work experience as formal project-professional training. PMI makes the final eligibility decision, including how an unusual course or academic program is treated.

What does not change

  • The requirement remains separate from exam readiness: completing eligible hours does not prove that a candidate is prepared to pass.
  • Self-paced training remains available: the new restriction is not a universal ATP requirement for every delivery format.
  • CAPM remains a route: an active CAPM certification can fulfill the PMP training requirement.
  • Documentation still matters: a marketing label cannot replace accurate dates, hours, content, and completion evidence.
  • CAPM policy is separate: this PMP live-training change should not be presented as a change to CAPM's own eligibility requirements.

Common questions

Must every PMP course come from an ATP after December 1?

No. The provider restriction applies to live instructor-led training. PMI's current PMP page separately permits online, on-demand self-paced courses from any organization.

Does buying a live course before December 1 preserve the old rule?

PMI's published transition language refers to training completed before the update. Purchase or enrollment is not the same as completion.

Does an expired CAPM fulfill the training requirement?

PMI identifies an active CAPM certification as the route. Confirm current certification status before relying on it.
