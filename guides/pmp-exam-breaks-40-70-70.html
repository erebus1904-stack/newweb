PMP Exam Breaks in 2026: Is the New Format Really 40-70-70?

Use the official section boundaries for exam-day decisions, and treat exact question counts as current observations until PMI publishes them.

Published and reviewed: September 4, 2026By PassGrid Editorial Team.Editorial Policy

Short answer

PMI officially confirms that the 2026 PMP exam has 180 questions, 240 minutes, and two 10-minute breaks. The first break occurs after the case-study section, and the second break occurs approximately midway through the independent-question portion. PMI does not publish or guarantee a fixed 40-70-70 question split. Recent candidate reports describe that layout, so it may help explain the current interface, but it is an operational observation rather than an official blueprint.

What does PMI officially say about PMP breaks?

The July 2026 PMP Examination Content Outline is the primary public source. It states that the exam includes 170 scored questions and 10 unscored pretest questions, for 180 total questions. Candidates receive 240 minutes of response time. The outline then provides two scheduled 10-minute breaks and ties them to sections rather than publishing fixed question numbers.

The first break follows the case-study section. The second break occurs approximately midway through the independent-question portion. Once you review your responses and start a break, you cannot return to questions from the previous section. That no-return rule matters more than whether a current exam form happens to display 40, 70, and 70 questions.

Where did 40-70-70 come from?

Recent candidates and instructors have described a first section of 40 questions followed by two sections of 70 questions. A current Reddit discussion includes a candidate report of that sequence and questions about how to pace each part. These accounts are useful for detecting an interface change, but they are not a PMI policy document, controlled sample, or guarantee that every delivery mode and exam form will remain identical.

The 40-70-70 split is not an official published or guaranteed PMP blueprint. It may represent the current operational layout, a staged rollout, or a layout that PMI can change without revising the Exam Content Outline. Prepare to recognize the reported structure, but make every irreversible exam decision from the section-review and break prompts shown on your own screen.

How should you budget the 240 minutes?

The 240-minute clock is shared across the exam sections; PMI does not assign a public fixed number of minutes to each section. Your pacing plan therefore needs checkpoints, not three separate countdown assumptions. Dividing 240 minutes by 180 questions produces an average of 80 seconds per question, but case sets, exhibits, matching items, and short independent questions will not consume equal time.

What should you do before each break?

  1. Confirm that every question in the current section has an answer.
  2. Review only marked items for which you have a specific unresolved reason.
  3. Do not reopen an answer merely because it feels uncomfortable; identify the fact that would justify changing it.
  4. Read the confirmation message before starting the break because the previous section will become inaccessible.
  5. Return within the displayed break limit and follow the proctor or testing-center instructions.

A break is a reset, not extra review time. Use it for water, movement, breathing, or the permitted personal needs described in your appointment instructions. Do not try to reconstruct or discuss questions from the closed section.

Are online and test-center breaks the same?

The ECO establishes the two-break structure for the PMP exam and directs online candidates to its Online Proctored Test guidance. An online candidate may be asked to present identification again after returning from a break. Test-center procedures for leaving the room, lockers, food, water, clothing, and re-entry depend on the center and the appointment instructions.

Do not infer those operational details from the 40-70-70 discussion. Whether you test online or at a test center, review the current PMI and Pearson VUE appointment instructions, complete any required system check, and follow the prompts presented by the live exam interface.

Common questions

Is the 2026 PMP exam officially divided into 40, 70, and 70 questions?

No official PMI document reviewed for this page publishes that fixed division. Candidates currently report seeing it, but PMI defines the break positions by the case-study and independent-question sections.

Can you return to questions after starting a break?

No. PMI says that after you review the section and start the break, you cannot return to questions in the previous section.

Does each PMP section have its own time limit?

PMI publishes 240 minutes of total response time, not a fixed public minute allowance for each section. Manage the remaining clock across the full exam.

Do the scheduled breaks use the 240 response minutes?

The ECO describes 240 minutes to respond to exam questions and separately provides two 10-minute breaks. Follow the live timer and do not exceed the displayed break period.

What if the exam screen does not match 40-70-70?

Follow the section and break prompts shown on your screen. The official section boundary and no-return warning take priority over a candidate report or training video.
