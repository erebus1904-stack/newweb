PMP exam update

Which PMP Study Materials Fit the July 2026 Exam?

Use your scheduled exam date and the current Exam Content Outline to decide whether a resource is current enough.

Last reviewed: September 1, 2026Updated with the confirmed December 1 training rule.

Short answer

If your PMP exam is on or after July 9, 2026, use the July 2026 PMP Exam Content Outline as the final coverage checklist. Older resources can still teach durable project-management principles, but they should not be your only guide because the refreshed exam changes domain emphasis and adds stronger focus on outcomes, value, AI, sustainability, and adaptive scenarios.

The post-launch routing rule

Exam before July 9, 2026

That exam window has passed. References to the prior domain weighting are now useful only for understanding older books, courses, score discussions, and study plans.

Exam on or after July 9, 2026

Use the July 2026 ECO. The published domain weights are People 33%, Process 41%, and Business Environment 26%.

The date on a video or book is only a warning signal. The stronger check is whether the material names the correct ECO, uses the current domain weights, and addresses the tasks and question formats shown in that outline.

What changed in study emphasis

The refreshed outline keeps the three domains but redistributes their weight. Business Environment rises substantially, while People and Process occupy smaller shares than in the prior outline. PMI also describes stronger emphasis on AI, sustainability, stakeholder engagement, outcomes, value, and real-world adaptive dynamics.

This does not mean every old concept became wrong. It means a resource can be accurate yet incomplete for the current exam. A strong study plan checks both conceptual quality and alignment with the current blueprint.

How deeply should you study a 35-hour PMP course?

A 35-hour course has two jobs: provide the education required for a PMP application and build a structured foundation for the current outline. Study deeply enough to explain the lesson objective, connect it to an ECO task, and apply it in a new scenario. Do not try to memorize every sentence, slide, or instructor phrase. Course completion alone does not prove that you can choose the next best action under time pressure.

After each lesson block, use a small mixed set to test transfer. If you miss because the concept is unfamiliar, return to the lesson or a trusted reference. If you know the concept but miss the role, project state, or decision sequence, practice scenarios instead of replaying the full course. PMI's official on-demand course is one way to satisfy the 35-hour requirement, but the same learning test applies to every course: documented hours, current ECO coverage, active retrieval, and timed application.

Training eligibility also needs a dated check. Starting December 1, 2026, PMI accepts live instructor-led hours only from a PMI Authorized Training Partner (ATP), China Registered Education Provider (REP), or eligible accredited higher-education program. Training completed before that date remains eligible under the earlier requirements, and self-paced courses may still come from any organization. The PMP 35-hour training rule guide explains the delivery-format distinction, active CAPM route, provider check, and records to keep.

For post-launch format observations, case-study preparation, Study Hall interpretation, and pacing, use the new PMP exam experience guide.

What older materials still teach well

Older resources may still be useful for stakeholder communication, team leadership, risk and issue handling, change control, planning, agile and hybrid delivery, procurement, governance, and benefits thinking. These are durable project-management areas, and scenario practice in them can remain valuable.

Keep an older resource when its explanations help you reason clearly. Supplement or replace it when its study schedule is built around the former 42% / 50% / 8% weighting, when it treats Business Environment as a small closing topic, or when it never tests the newer emphasis described in the July 2026 ECO.

Build a coverage matrix before buying another course

List the July 2026 domains and tasks in one column, then write where each current resource teaches and tests them. Mark a row as covered only when you can point to a lesson, a worked example, and at least one practice activity. A course title or chapter label alone is weak evidence because the material may mention a topic without developing the judgment the exam expects.

Use three labels: covered, partial, and missing. Covered means you can explain the task and apply it in a scenario. Partial means the resource introduces the topic but lacks current examples or practice. Missing means you cannot locate usable instruction. This matrix prevents two expensive mistakes: replacing a good foundation resource merely because it is older, and assuming a newly marketed resource covers the entire outline merely because it says “2026.”

Update the plan without restarting from zero

If you began with older materials, preserve completed work that still maps to the current outline. Do not restart every chapter automatically. Instead, move the uncovered and partial rows in your matrix to the front of the next study cycle. Rebalance practice so Business Environment receives attention consistent with the current outline, and add scenarios that connect project choices to value, outcomes, organizational context, AI, sustainability, and stakeholder consequences.

After each repair block, answer a mixed set without topic labels. A gap is not repaired merely because the new explanation feels familiar. You should be able to identify the relevant signal, explain the decision owner, choose a responsible next action, and connect the choice to value or project outcomes. Keep a short note showing which ECO task the exercise repaired; that creates a review trail instead of another pile of disconnected scores.

Check format readiness separately from topic readiness

Knowing the content and navigating the question format are different skills. The July 2026 ECO describes multiple item formats, including scenario-based material. Use official descriptions to understand the formats, then practice reading exhibits, separating relevant from distracting details, and carrying one scenario across related questions. Do not assume a third-party simulator reproduces the live interface exactly.

Track format errors separately in your mistake log. “I did not know the concept” requires content repair. “I missed a condition in the case,” “I changed a correct answer after rereading,” or “I lost the decision owner across several questions” requires a reading and pacing adjustment. This distinction keeps a format problem from triggering unnecessary chapter review.

PassGrid coverage and limits

These materials can support current preparation, but PassGrid does not claim that every existing question has been individually audited against every task in the July 2026 ECO.
