Project management basics and exam language PMBOK Guide Section 1; Process Groups Practice Guide overview

Knowledge points

  • Project, program, portfolio, operations, governance, PMO, sponsor, customer, project team.
  • How the PMBOK standard relates to the PMBOK Guide.
  • Why PMP questions test judgment instead of isolated definitions.

Key focus

  • Know what a project manager can decide and when authority belongs to the sponsor, product owner, PMO, or change authority.
  • Read the situation first, then choose the next action.

Value delivery, project principles, and delivery approach signals PMBOK Standard Sections 2-4; Agile Practice Guide life cycle context

Knowledge points

  • Value creation, project environment, enterprise environmental factors, organizational process assets.
  • Project management principles, systems thinking, quality, leadership, sustainability, and team enablement.
  • Predictive, adaptive, and hybrid life cycles, using the Agile Practice Guide for adaptive delivery signals.

Key focus

  • Identify whether the question is predictive, agile, or hybrid before choosing an action.
  • Connect value delivery to stakeholder decisions, change control, and project success.

Governance, business analysis, scope, schedule, and financial performance PMBOK Guide 2.1-2.4; Process Groups Practice Guide; Business Analysis Practice Guide requirements topics

Knowledge points

  • Governance model, value creation, project controls, scope definition, requirements, WBS, acceptance criteria.
  • Needs assessment, requirements elicitation, requirements analysis, traceability, and product scope versus project scope.
  • Schedule planning, dependencies, critical path, cost estimates, budget, baseline, and performance measurement.

Key focus

  • When approved scope, schedule, cost, or baseline may change, evaluate impact and use formal change control.
  • Do not jump directly to crashing, fast tracking, approval, or rejection without analyzing the constraint.

Stakeholders, resources, team behavior, and risk PMBOK Guide 2.5-2.7; Process Groups Practice Guide stakeholder, resource, and risk processes

Knowledge points

  • Stakeholder identification, analysis, engagement, communication, team resources, conflict, responsibility, risk, and issue handling.
  • Process group flow for planning, executing, monitoring, and controlling stakeholder, resource, and risk work.
  • Stakeholder register, stakeholder engagement plan, communication management plan, risk register, risk report, and issue log.

Key focus

  • For stakeholder resistance, analyze and communicate before escalating.
  • Separate risks from issues. Risks are uncertain future events; issues have already happened.

Process group flow from initiating to closing Process Groups Practice Guide: initiating, planning, executing, monitoring and controlling, closing

Knowledge points

  • Project charter, stakeholder register, project management plan, subsidiary plans, baselines, work performance data, change requests, and final transition.
  • How initiating, planning, executing, monitoring and controlling, and closing connect across integration, scope, schedule, cost, quality, resources, communications, risk, procurement, and stakeholders.

Key focus

  • For "what should the project manager do next" questions, identify the process group before choosing an action.
  • Do not execute a solution when the scenario still requires planning, analysis, impact assessment, or formal change control.
Business analysis, requirements, traceability, and acceptance Business Analysis Practice Guide: needs assessment, requirements work, traceability, and solution evaluation

Knowledge points

  • Needs assessment, business case, stakeholder analysis, requirements planning, elicitation, analysis, prioritization, traceability, acceptance criteria, and solution evaluation.
  • Product scope versus project scope, requirement changes, requirement validation, and how business value connects to project decisions.

Key focus

  • When a question mentions unclear needs, changing requirements, acceptance disputes, or product value, slow down and analyze the requirement path before acting.
  • Trace requirements from business need to deliverable acceptance; do not treat scope, requirement, and benefit as the same thing.

Know which plan, register, artifact, or tool to use PMBOK Guide Sections 3-5; Process Groups Practice Guide artifacts, tools, and techniques

Knowledge points

  • Tailoring, life cycle selection, continuous improvement, project management plans, subsidiary plans, registers, logs, reports, baselines, deliverables.
  • Process inputs, outputs, tools, and techniques as practical decision aids, not isolated memorization lists.
  • Meetings, expert judgment, data analysis, decision making, estimation, facilitation, audit, and retrospective techniques.

Key focus

  • Do not memorize every input and output. Learn which artifact fits the scenario.
  • For change, look for change request, change log, impact assessment, approval authority, and baseline.

Scrum, Kanban, backlog flow, iteration review, and retrospectives Agile Practice Guide: Scrum, Kanban, backlog flow, reviews, and retrospectives

Knowledge points

  • Product owner, Scrum Master, self-organizing team, daily scrum, sprint review, retrospective, product backlog, sprint backlog, Kanban, and WIP.
  • How change enters a backlog and how priority is managed in adaptive work.

Key focus

  • The product owner owns value and priority; the Scrum Master removes impediments and supports the team.
  • Agile change is usually refined and prioritized in the backlog, not forced into the current sprint by the project manager.

CAPM to PMP bridge.

PMP and CAPM share a project management knowledge base, but PMP practice should move from concept recognition into scenario judgment, role authority, value delivery, and timing discipline. If you are moving from CAPM to PMP, use the CAPM Study Hub to repair fundamentals and the CAPM to PMP study bridge to decide when to switch into PMP scenario practice.
